How to use this book with TSK

The book is a companion to Tan, Steinbach, Karpatne, and Kumar, written to be read a chapter a week beside it, and it does not replace it. Each week, read the TSK material first and this book's chapter second. The table below says, for every chapter, what the canonical material is, what the observation reading adds, which instrument or audit the chapter introduces, and what the chapter's evidence licenses a reader to conclude and what it does not. Two chapters, 5 and 7, are standard content with one observation sidebar each, and say so in their first paragraph.

Licensed, and not licensed. The last column uses two words in the program's own sense. A claim is licensed when it is a theorem checked in Lean in appendix C, or a measurement that was sealed with its commit hash before the run and survived its registered null and bar, and the chapter may rest its thesis on it. A claim is not licensed when it is exploratory, a retrospective fit, a single seed, or a posited extension of a measured one, and the chapter may mention it and may not lean on it. The classes in between, demonstrated, replicated, and predicted, are defined in chapter 8 section 8.10, and a row of the table names which one each claim carries. Chapters 3, 4, 8, 11, 12, and 13 carry most of the book's thesis. Chapter 0 is the mathematics toolkit, primers L and S rebuild the first courses beneath it, and none of the three makes an empirical claim.

A reader who wants only the thesis can read chapters 1, 3, 4, 8, and 11 in that order. A reader teaching from TSK can assign chapters 5 and 7 as optional and lose no thread. Every chapter's exercises assume the TSK material of its row has been read.
