Data Mining as Observation

A companion to Tan, Steinbach, Karpatne, and Kumar, with Observation Theory as its spine. Draft 0.3, 2026-09-09. Standard content is cited to Tan, Steinbach, Karpatne, Kumar and to Hastie, Tibshirani, Friedman. Every number in the book traces to a committed artifact listed at the end of its chapter.

Online edition of draft 0.3, built from commit f3914f0. Copyright © 2026 Andrew H. Bond. All rights reserved. The answer key, appendix B, is not part of the online edition. The book’s terms are defined, with their evidence, in the Observation Theory Encyclopedia.

Preface

This book teaches a one-semester graduate course in data mining, in the week order of ECE 514 at Sonoma State University, with one idea running through every week. The quality of a representation, a distance, a reduction, an index, or an evaluation is not a property of the object. It is a property of the relation between the object and the consumer that reads it, under a budget. The idea has a name, Observation Theory, and a theory book of its own, cited throughout as Volume 14. This is not that book. It cites Volume 14 for proofs and the standard texts, Tan, Steinbach, Karpatne, and Kumar and Hastie, Tibshirani, and Friedman, for the classical content, and it never rewrites either. Its own pages are three things per chapter. The observer reading of that week's topic. The instruments the reading produced. The failures that earned it.

The cover is the book's one idea drawn to scale and left without a label, so that this paragraph is the only place it is explained. Two error distributions share the page. The first has variance 0.3 along the horizontal axis and 1.7 along the vertical, the second is the reverse, and each is drawn as its ellipse one standard deviation out, blue for the first and orange for the second. Their traces, the sums of their variances, are both 2.0, so mean squared error, total variance, and every other measure that does not name a reader calls them the same size. Two readers cross the picture as dashed lines through the centre, one at 15 degrees from horizontal and one at 75. A reader that reads a single direction experiences an error as the error's shadow on its line, and the half-length of that shadow is the square root of the read distortion of equation 3.1, $u^{\top}\Sigma u$, the variance of the error along the direction read. The dotted lines drop from the point of each ellipse that lies farthest along a reader's direction to that reader's line, and the heavy bars beside each line are the shadows themselves. The reader at 15 degrees sees the first error as 0.39 and the second as 1.61. The reader at 75 degrees sees the first as 1.61 and the second as 0.39. Each reader ranks the pair about four to one, and the two readers rank it the opposite way, while nothing about either error changed. The ranking belongs to the pair of error and reader, and that sentence is what the fourteen data-mining chapters that follow spend their pages earning, on attention keys, seismograms, legal opinions, database replicas, and radio channels. Every number on the cover is computed by the script that draws it, tools/make_cover.py, from the two variances and the two angles, and chapter 3 section 3.2 works the same example by hand.

Every number in the book traces to a committed artifact. Each chapter ends with a table naming, for each number, the repository, file, and line range it came from, and where one exists, a commit hash or a DOI. Failures are printed at the prominence of the results they bound. Refuted bars, retracted results, and vacuous passes are chapter material rather than footnotes, because a claim that cannot fail cannot inform.

Chapter 0 introduces every mathematical tool the book uses beyond a first course in linear algebra and a first course in statistics, each with a small example that can be checked by hand. Two primers before it, L for linear algebra and S for probability and statistics, rebuild those first courses from arithmetic for the reader who needs them, in the order the book uses the ideas and with the same hand-checked examples. Every chapter ends with three tiers of exercises, warm-up, by hand with an answer key, and programming, and one homework aligned to the course calendar with a preregistration step that is graded on having been made rather than on being right. The exercise notebooks, the toolkit module they import, the figures and the scripts that draw them, and the consistency checker that keeps the cross-references true all live in the book's repository.

Two chapters, 5 and 7, are standard content with one sidebar each, and the book says so rather than stretch. The rest carry the thesis, and six of them, 3, 4, 8, 11, 12, and 13, carry most of it.
