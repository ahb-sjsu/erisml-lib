Chapter 4. Dimensionality reduction as consumer-relative compression

Data Mining as Observation, draft 0.3, 2026-09-09. Week 4 of ECE 514. Reading due before this chapter is TSK Appendix B [1] and a review of eigendecomposition. Every number below traces to a committed file listed in the last section.

Week three ended with the curse of dimensionality and two escapes from it. Select the features you have, or make new ones. This chapter is about the second escape, and about the sentence in the session deck that the whole book hangs on. Reduction is lossy compression, and what your downstream task reads decides what you may discard.

The standard treatment derives principal components from variance. Find the direction of largest spread, then the largest spread orthogonal to it, and keep the first few. That derivation is correct and the reader should be able to reproduce it. What it hides is an assumption. It assumes that spread is interest, that a direction in which the data varies a lot is a direction worth keeping. For a consumer that reads every direction equally, that assumption is right. For any other consumer it is a bet, and this chapter measures what the bet costs when it loses.

4.1 Reduction is compression, and compression has a reader

Take a table of rows as vectors of dimension d. Any reduction to r dimensions and back is a code. It produces an approximation of each row, and the difference between the row and its approximation is the code's error. Principal components are the code that minimizes the total squared error at a given r. That is a theorem, and it is the theorem the session's scree plot is built on.

Total squared error weighs every direction of the error equally. Chapter 1 wrote the consumer's distortion as the error read through the consumer, and chapter 3 showed a reader at fifteen degrees ranking two equal-trace errors four to one. Principal components are the optimal code for the reader that weighs all directions equally, which is to say for no particular reader at all. The operative distortion for a real consumer is the trace of the consumer's read operator times the error's second-moment matrix, its covariance when the error is centered. Section 0.5 defines the read operator as the consumer's average sensitivity to each direction, section 0.3 defines the trace, and their product is the error's total variance weighted by how much the consumer reads each direction. When the read operator is the identity, that is total squared error and principal components are optimal. When it is anything else, the variance ordering can put the consumer's most sensitive direction last.

$$d_O=\operatorname{tr}(P_C\,M_\delta)\qquad\text{against}\qquad \operatorname{tr}M_\delta=d_O\big|_{P_C=I}. \tag{4.1}$$

That explained variance is the fraction a consumer with unit sensitivities retains, and that with variances 9 and 1 a consumer reading only the second direction retains nothing of a one-component reduction that explains nine tenths, are machine-checked in Lean in appendix C.

That in the eigenbasis the variance along a unit direction lies between the smallest and the largest eigenvalue, and that the error of keeping $k$ components is the sum of the dropped eigenvalues, are machine-checked in Lean in appendix C.

That the reconstruction error is zero exactly when the approximation is the row, and that for a projection onto a unit direction it is the squared length less the squared component along it, are machine-checked in Lean in appendix C.

The session's live demonstration reconstructs a dataset from its leading components and inspects what was lost. This chapter's version of that demonstration asks a second question. Lost to whom.

4.2 Bits go where the consumer is sensitive, not where the variance is

Fix a bit budget and a set of directions. Each direction has a source variance, how much the data spreads along it, and a sensitivity, how much the consumer's output moves when that direction is perturbed. The distortion the consumer feels is, for a uniform quantizer at high rate in the covariance's own eigenbasis, the sum over directions of sensitivity times variance times a factor that falls by four for each bit spent on that direction, since a bit halves the rounding error and quarters its square. Both the variance $\sigma_i^2$ and the sensitivity $s_i$ are read in the covariance's eigenbasis $v_i$, the sensitivity along the $i$th eigenvector being $v_i^{\top}P_C v_i$, so that the two numbers describe the same direction. The eigenvalues of the read operator and of the covariance can be paired only when the two share an eigenbasis, and the formula does not assume they do. That is the approximation of chapter 0 section 0.7, and the closed form below is a theorem only under those two conditions. Minimizing that sum under a fixed total number of bits gives each direction half the base-two logarithm of its sensitivity-weighted variance over a water level, or zero if that quantity is below the water. Directions the consumer does not read, or the data does not populate, get no bits at all. Section 0.7 works a three-direction example by hand.

$$D(b)=\sum_i s_i\sigma_i^{2}\,2^{-2b_i},\qquad s_i=v_i^{\top}P_C\,v_i,\qquad b_i^{\star}=\max\!\Big(0,\ \tfrac12\log_2\frac{s_i\sigma_i^{2}}{\theta}\Big),\qquad \sum_i b_i^{\star}=B, \tag{4.2}$$

This is reverse water-filling, the same optimization as power allocation across frequency channels [5, 15], with a task's sensitivity in place of a signal's power. The measurement instrument of chapter 11 ships it as a function that takes the sensitivities along the covariance's eigenvectors, $v_i^{\top}P_C v_i$, the source variances, and a budget, and returns the allocation and the number of directions it starved. The compression library wraps it in a report [12] that states the gain over uniform allocation at the same budget, which is the number that says what consumer-awareness bought.

The report also carries a caution, and the caution is the reason chapter 11 exists. When the read operator's effective rank, the cutoff-free count of how many directions it uses defined in section 0.4, falls below two, the allocation pours everything into one or two directions. That is correct if the operator is correct. It is also exactly the signature a sub-dimensional probe produces regardless of what the consumer reads, because recovery of a read operator is a cliff at full dimension and the cliff is rank-independent. A concentrated allocation is either the right answer or the fingerprint of an under-budgeted measurement, and the report cannot tell which. The reader has to check how the operator was obtained.

The saving, measured end to end. [13] A governed run built three coders for a nonlinear consumer that reads four of eight directions. An output coder allocates against the consumer's true distortion. A surrogate coder allocates against the trace of the read operator times the error. A reconstruction coder allocates against total squared error. At every rate the ordering held. Output distortion at or below surrogate, surrogate at or below reconstruction. At high rate the reconstruction coder was up to about 500 times worse on the consumer's metric, because its bits went to the four directions the consumer never reads. The relative distance between the surrogate and the output coder shrank from 0.41 to 0.005 as the budget grew, so that at high resolution a surrogate for the read operator suffices and at low resolution it does not. The isotropic control, a consumer that reads all eight directions, collapsed the three coders to one, as it must. When the consumer reads everything there is nothing to save.

$$R_C(D)=\min_{p(\hat x\mid x)\,:\ \operatorname{tr}(P_C M_\delta)\le D}\ I(x;\hat x). \tag{4.3}$$

Two closed forms say what mismatch costs. Coding for a read operator of rank M when the consumer reads rank m below M costs excess rate of order half the read rank times the log of M over m. That is the price of protecting directions nobody reads. The dangerous mismatch runs the other way. A code blind to a direction the consumer reads does not cost extra bits. It hits a floor that no budget descends below, equal to the read operator's weight on the omitted direction after whitening, the rescaling of section 0.4 that gives every direction of the data variance one. A first draft claimed the distortion was unbounded, and a fresh-context pass corrected it to a floor. A naive kernel projector that ignores the source covariance overstates the floor by about 30 percent, and that was corrected too. Both corrections are in the ledger at the prominence of the theorem.

$$\Delta R\approx\frac r2\log\frac Mm,\qquad D\ \ge\ D_{\mathrm{floor}}=\operatorname{tr}\big(\tilde P\,\Pi\big),\qquad \tilde P=\Sigma_x^{1/2}P_C\,\Sigma_x^{1/2}. \tag{4.4}$$

That a uniform quantizer's error is at most half a step, that a per-channel quantizer's squared error is at most the sum of the squared half steps, and that a larger codebook never increases distortion, are machine-checked in Lean in appendix C. Those are the identity reader's bounds.

The floor was then measured on a trained language model. On sixteen of sixteen attention heads, the units of a language model that section 0.11 introduces, the read distortion saturated at the measured whitened value, a naive assumption of identity covariance was off by 20 to 60 percent, and a coder allocating against the wrong operator plateaued at a divergence between its attention output and the uncompressed output, measured in nats (section 0.11), about 100000 times the matched coder's, median 0.10 against about 6e-7. The first sealed finite-rate gate for that measurement missed, zero of sixteen, because the divergence was still improving at the top swept rate and the sweep was too shallow to distinguish reaching the floor from the floor existing. The gate flaw was logged and the claim was resolved by a fresh held-out test, not by relaxing the bar.

4.3 At matched reconstruction the better code loses

The empirical heart of the observation program is called the flip [13], and it is the sentence in the session deck made falsifiable. Fix a budget. Build two codes for the same data. One preserves what the consumer reads. The other is reconstruction-optimal. The flip is the conjunction of two sealed inequalities on held-out data at matched bits. The read-preserving code beats the reconstruction-optimal code on the consumer's task, and the reconstruction-optimal code reconstructs the data better. Either alone is unremarkable. Together they say that the code which is worse by the universal yardstick is better by the only yardstick that ships. A third arm that deliberately destroys the read direction must be worst downstream, or the read operator is not what governs the task.

$$\text{flip}:\quad \mathrm{task}(O)>\mathrm{task}(R)\ \ \text{and}\ \ \operatorname{tr}M^{O}_\delta>\operatorname{tr}M^{R}_\delta,\qquad \mathrm{task}(\text{anti})<\mathrm{task}(R),\qquad \text{bits}(O)=\text{bits}(R). \tag{4.5}$$

The dissociation, cleanly. On transformer attention keys at audit-matched bits, two arms with reconstruction error 0.0934 and 0.0938, a relative mismatch of 0.4 percent within the 2 percent tolerance the program's matched-distortion cells register, differed 2.53 times on the downstream metric in twelve of twelve heads, and the anti arm was 21 times worse. Because the arm that won on the consumer reconstructed slightly worse, the downstream difference cannot be a reconstruction effect in its favour. The positive half, that the controlling quantity is the read distortion, then replicated on softmax attention with a symmetric flip and on embedding retrieval at identical reconstruction error 0.0964, where the two read-preserving arms scored negative 4.70 and positive 4.65 against each other's consumers, twelve of twelve both ways, while reconstruction predicted nothing at 0.40. Two representations, two consumers, one mechanism.

The flip travels. The program then put it at risk in twelve domains and three branches of physics, each configuration frozen on a calibration split and scored on held-out data with outcomes reported regardless of sign.

The battery's own sealed prediction, a flip in at least two of three core prospective domains, was met by the acoustic and seismic rows. Across anchors and confirmations the flip holds in at least five independent domains and three distinct physics, electromagnetic, acoustic, and elastic, plus attention, classifiers, and retrieval.

Why it works. The read-preserving code spends its bits in the read subspace. The reconstruction-optimal code spreads them to be small on average and deposits error where the consumer looks. The best single code the program found whitens the source (section 0.4) before allocating, so that the code's error aligns with the read operator's geometry. On whale codas it won at every bit level, 0.83, 0.85, and 0.97 against 0.41, 0.80, and 0.89 for a per-channel code. No single code wins everywhere. Whitening wins where the source is cross-channel correlated, a generic polar code where it is isotropic, and a per-channel code where the structure is channel-aligned. That is why the engineering answer is an adaptive selector rather than a universal code.

For the session's PCA demonstration this is the second question answered. What was lost was the consumer's read subspace, whenever the read subspace is not the high-variance subspace, and the flip says that at matched bits the loss is not small.

4.4 The flip has a boundary, and the boundary is measurable before compressing

A claim that cannot fail cannot inform, and the flip fails in a nameable place. It needs a read operator that is misaligned with where the signal has its energy. When the two are intrinsically coupled it cannot appear. Gradient compression against a curvature consumer on a real trained model showed the anti arm worst 300 of 300 times, so the read operator governs the task, and a flip only 27 percent of the time, because in a trained model the high-curvature directions are the high-gradient-energy directions. That row is carried as refuted as a flip and as a genuine boundary, not a missing measurement.

The program turned the boundary into a dial. Define the alignment as the normalized overlap between the average read operator and the source covariance on the read subspace. It is small when the consumer reads directions the signal is quiet in and approaches one when the read subspace is the high-energy subspace. The alignment law is the claim that flip magnitude decreases in that number, with alignment one as the no-flip null. Across the twelve measured domains the retrospective fit is decreasing, with the gradient-compression row sitting at the null exactly as predicted. The retrospective fit is an exploratory row and the program forbids its umbrella claims from leaning on it. One sealed prospective point at intermediate alignment is the confirmatory test.

$$\kappa=\operatorname{tr}\big(\bar P_C\,\bar\Sigma_x\big)\in[0,1],\qquad \kappa\to1\ \text{is the coupling null (no flip)}. \tag{4.6}$$

That the alignment lies in the unit interval, that it equals one when the read operator is a positive multiple of the covariance, and that in that case no flip is possible, are machine-checked in Lean for diagonal operators in two dimensions in appendix C.

For a data-mining project the dial is operational. Measure the overlap between what the model reads and where the data varies before compressing, and you have a prediction of whether reduction by variance will hurt, without running the downstream task.

Every verdict is also budget-relative. On real book embeddings, a spectral quality statistic rose with corpus size when the number of modes was held at ten and collapsed when the number of modes grew with the corpus to 121, 126, and 159, across three seeds, converging at the minimum corpus size. Same data, opposite verdict, set by the observer's budget. A better code without a named budget is as unanswerable as a better code without a named consumer.

4.5 Truncation is a bet on the spectrum

The session's scree plot asks how many components to keep. The compression library's public benchmark answers it with a table and a rule [12].

On a standard 100-dimensional word-embedding corpus of 1.18 million vectors, scored against provided ground-truth neighbours over 2000 queries at about 16 times compression.

methodbytes per vectorrecall at 10, one stagerecall at 10, with rerank

full 100 dimensions, 2-bit scalar, matched bytes250.5740.906

With the library's default truncation the method loses to product quantization, 0.685 against 0.862. The diagnostic says why. Sixty-four components retain only 73 percent of this corpus's variance, so truncation discards real signal. On 768-dimensional sentence embeddings, truncation to 256 keeps about 99 percent, and there the same method reaches recall 0.999. With no truncation at the same byte budget the scalar quantizer beats both product quantizers on the word embeddings, 0.906 against 0.862 and 0.866. The failure was truncating data that is not truncatable.

The rule the benchmark draws is the identity reader's rule for the scree plot, and the book records it as the benchmark's rule rather than as its own. Pick the rank from the cumulative explained-variance curve, at 95 percent or above, never from a fixed dimension. On compact descriptor sets that means little or no truncation. On high-dimensional embeddings with concentrated spectra it means aggressive truncation. The library's claims ledger records this beside its headline, marks the truncation claim reproducible, and states that on compact sets it loses.

One caveat sits beside the rule. Explained variance is the identity-reader's criterion. The 95 percent threshold protects the variance a consumer that reads everything would need. Sections 4.2 and 4.3 say that a consumer which reads a quiet direction is not protected by any variance threshold, and the flip is what that looks like. The scree plot is the right tool for the identity reader and a bet for every other one.

4.6 Five proxies that were refuted on the way to the right statistic

The read distortion did not arrive as a hypothesis. It was found by refuting its neighbours, and the ledger keeps every step.

An online recalibration step improved reconstruction on every window and was worse on the consumer's metric on every window. That is the trade of section 4.3 in its purest form.

The first registered test of the flip missed two of four bars, and the apparent reversal was a matched-bits confound, an uncounted per-block codebook adding about 25 percent to one arm's bits. The refutation was of the test, not the claim, and it forced a bit-matched redesign.

A demeaned error-norm proxy failed to order the arms. The controlling quantity was the across-token variance of the query-projected error, not any scalar of error magnitude.

At fixed reconstruction, the downstream ranking of two codes flipped with the consumer. Preservation is a property of the code and consumer as a pair, not of the code.

A variance-ratio proxy reached only Spearman 0.80 against the downstream metric. It was a noisy linear estimate of the read distortion, and the direct projection replaced it.

The direct read distortion nailed the discriminating flip twelve of twelve and still misordered one middle pair whose per-token error had structure the trace does not see. The mechanism is real and the scalar is not a complete rank statistic.

A prospective attempt on an independent representation found no flip, because one arm reconstructed strictly better and dominated downstream. The precondition that came out of it is that the flip is observable only for reconstruction-matched arms, and every later flip row is built on matched arms. The miss defined the protocol.

Seven sealed misses, each carried, each narrowing the claim, converging on a statistic that is load-bearing and bounded. A data-mining project that compares two reductions will meet the same ladder in miniature. The bits must be matched, the reconstruction must be matched, the anti arm must be run, and the scalar that finally predicts the downstream result must be reported with the cases it misorders.

4.7 Feature selection inside the fold is the same rule

The session's second half turns to selection. Filter methods score each feature against the label by correlation, mutual information, or a chi-square statistic and keep the top scorers. Wrapper methods add or remove features by refitting the model. Embedded methods let a penalty or a tree's split statistics do the selecting. Each has blind spots the deck names, and one rule governs all of them. Select inside the cross-validation fold or leak.

Chapter 8 gives that rule its mechanics with ESL 7.10.2 [2], where selecting one hundred of five thousand noise predictors on all fifty rows before cross-validating reports three percent error against a true fifty. The observer version is one sentence. Selection on all rows lets the evaluation harness read the test fold. The read operator of the harness then overlaps a direction the deployed model will never have, and the number it reports is about the harness.

Selection and extraction differ in one way that matters for this chapter. A selected feature is a coordinate the consumer can name. An extracted component is a direction the consumer reads through a linear map. When the consumer's read operator is known, the selection question has a direct answer. Keep the coordinates with the largest sensitivity-weighted variance, which is section 4.2's water-filling restricted to axis-aligned directions. When it is not known, the importance scores from trees and penalties are a proxy for it, and the deck's warning about importance pathologies is the warning that a proxy for the read operator is not the read operator.

4.8 What a reduction must state

A project report that reduces dimension states the following beside the reduction.

  • The consumer. Which downstream step reads the reduced vectors, and whether its read operator is known, estimated, or assumed to be the identity.
  • The variance retained and the sensitivity retained. The scree-plot number, and beside it the fraction of the consumer's sensitivity-weighted variance that survives, if the read operator is available.
  • Matched bits for any comparison of two reductions, with the reconstruction error of each arm reported so that a flip can be recognized as one.
  • The anti arm. For any claim that reduction preserved what the consumer reads, a control that destroys the read direction and is confirmed worst. In the two-dimensional diagonal setting of chapter 3, that no code of a given total error can be read as more than the total, and that the code which puts the whole error on the read direction is read as exactly that, are machine-checked in Lean in appendix C.
  • The budget. Every verdict is stated at a named budget, because at a different budget the ranking can invert.
  • The alignment, if measured, between the read subspace and the high-variance subspace, so that a reader can predict whether variance-based reduction was safe.

A report that keeps components by explained variance and does not name the consumer has made the identity-reader bet. The report may make it. It must say so.

Exercises

Warm-up

  1. State the assumption under which principal components are the optimal reduction, in terms of the read operator.
  2. Explain why a direction with large variance can receive zero bits under water-filling.
  3. What is the difference between the commission tax and the omission floor, and which one has no remedy at any budget?
  4. Define the flip in two inequalities and state why either alone is unremarkable.
  5. Why must every flip row be built on reconstruction-matched arms?
  6. Give one reason an allocation concentrated on a single direction may be correct and one reason it may be an artifact.
  7. Why did the isotropic control in the governed run collapse the three coders to one?
  8. Explain in one sentence why a scree plot protects the identity reader and no other.
  9. The gradient-compression row shows the anti arm worst 300 of 300 times and a flip only 27 percent of the time. Explain how both can be true.
  10. State the rule that governs feature selection inside cross-validation and the observer-language reason for it.

By hand

  1. A dataset has covariance with diagonal (4, 1) and no correlation. The consumer reads only the second coordinate. Principal components keep one direction. State which direction is kept, the fraction of total variance retained, and the fraction of the consumer's sensitivity-weighted variance retained.
  2. Three directions have sensitivities (1, 0, 0.25) and variances (1, 4, 4). The budget is 2 bits. Compute the water-filling allocation and state which direction with the largest variance is starved.
  3. A code protects a read operator of rank 8 when the consumer reads rank 2 and the read rank r is 4. Using base-two logarithms, compute the commission tax in bits.
  4. A read operator has eigenvalues (10, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1). Compute its effective rank and state whether the allocation report would issue its caution.
  5. Two arms have reconstruction error 0.10 and 0.10, downstream scores 0.8 and 0.5, and the anti arm scores 0.3. State whether this is a flip under the chapter's definition, and if not, which inequality is missing.
  6. A reduction keeps 95 percent of variance. The consumer's read subspace lies entirely in the discarded 5 percent. State the fraction of the consumer's sensitivity-weighted variance retained, and name the theorem that says no budget recovers it.

Programming

  1. Reproduce the session's demonstration on a dataset from your project. Reconstruct from the leading components, then train the downstream model on the reconstruction and on the original. Report the explained variance kept and the downstream metric lost, side by side, at three ranks.
  2. Estimate your downstream model's read operator by finite differences of its score with respect to the input, averaged over a sample of rows, at a budget of twice the input dimension. Report its effective rank and compare it to the rank you kept in exercise 17.
  3. Run the water-filling allocation against that operator at a fixed bit budget and against a uniform allocation. Report the predicted gain over uniform. If the effective rank is below two, state how the operator was obtained before trusting the split.
  4. Build two reductions of the same data at matched bits, one by variance and one against the read operator. Report reconstruction error and the downstream metric for each. State whether a flip occurred, and run the anti arm.
  5. Plot the cumulative explained-variance curve for your embedding corpus. State the rank at 95 percent. State whether your corpus is truncatable by the benchmark's rule.
  6. Repeat exercise 20 at half and double the bit budget. State whether the ranking of the two codes changed.
  7. Select features by correlation with the label on all rows, then cross-validate. Repeat with selection inside each fold. Report both error rates and name the read direction the first harness had that the second did not.
  8. Compute the alignment of section 4.4, the normalized overlap between your read operator and your data covariance on the read subspace. State whether the value predicts a flip, then check the prediction against exercise 20.

Answers to the warm-up and by-hand problems are collected in the answer key at the end of the book.
