pairwise interaction matrix concept

DefinitionLet $\Delta_i$ be the rise in error from removing dimension i alone. The pairwise interaction $\Phi_{ij}$ is the rise from removing both i and j, minus $\Delta_i + \Delta_j$. A positive value marks a redundant pair, each standing in for the other. A negative value marks a complementary pair, each needing the other.
ExampleIn the volume’s illustration, two copies of one signal cost 0 to remove singly and 1 to remove together, so the interaction is 1 - 0 = +1. Two halves of an exclusive-or cost 1 each and 1 together, so it is 1 - 2 = -1.

Assumptions and scope
  • The matrix is symmetric with zero diagonal and costs 1 + n + n(n-1)/2 evaluations for n dimensions.
  • Ablation from the full model is blind to redundant pairs, and adding dimensions one at a time is blind to complementary pairs.
  • The two identities of Section 12.4.2 that tie the matrix to the gains of a build-up sequence hold exactly only when the evaluation function has no interactions above second order.
  • The sign convention was corrected in the November 2026 review. The matrix printed in Section 12.2.2 is hypothetical.
