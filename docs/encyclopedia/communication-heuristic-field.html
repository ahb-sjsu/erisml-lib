communication heuristic field concept

DefinitionA smooth function h on the communication manifold that gives, at each point, the estimated cost of reaching the intended communicative act. Its negative gradient points toward the interpretations the heuristic judges more likely. It is admissible if it never overestimates the distance to the intended meaning.
ExampleIf the true cost to the intended reading is 5, an estimate of 3 is admissible and an estimate of 7 is not. In the volume’s illustration, hearing the word bank in a talk about fishing gives a steep descent toward the riverbank reading.

Assumptions and scope
  • Interpretation is modelled as search on the manifold. The hearer is given the signal and partial information about pragmatics and content.
  • The field is not static. The chapter says it is assembled from at least four sources, among them linguistic convention and discourse context.
  • An admissible heuristic may underestimate ambiguity. It is consistent only if it also satisfies the triangle inequality \(h(m_1) \le d(m_1, m_2) + h(m_2)\).
  • The formalism is inherited from Geometric Reasoning. No heuristic field is estimated in this volume.
Prior artAdmissible and consistent heuristics of A* search, and the principle of charity of Grice and Davidson, which the chapter names.
