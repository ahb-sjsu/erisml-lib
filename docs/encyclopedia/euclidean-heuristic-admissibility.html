admissibility of the Euclidean heuristic under a Mahalanobis metric result

DefinitionIn A* search with Mahalanobis edge weights, the straight-line Euclidean distance to the goal never overestimates the true cost exactly when every eigenvalue of the inverse covariance is at least 1. Otherwise scale it by the square root of the smallest eigenvalue, or use the Mahalanobis distance to the goal as the heuristic.
ExampleIn the volume’s posited covariance the Consequences axis has variance 25, so a Euclidean step of 1 along it has Mahalanobis length 0.2. The Euclidean heuristic says 1 and overestimates. For a diagonal covariance the matching eigenvalue of the inverse is 0.04, and scaling by its square root gives 0.2.

Assumptions and scope
  • The metric tensor is a constant inverse covariance, so the space is flat and anisotropic, not curved.
  • The scaled heuristic is admissible and often very loose. The Mahalanobis heuristic is exact for one step and ignores boundary penalties, so it can fall far below the true cost where a boundary lies on the path.
  • The covariance the chapter applies it to, with variances from 0.25 to 25, is posited. No record supports its values.
