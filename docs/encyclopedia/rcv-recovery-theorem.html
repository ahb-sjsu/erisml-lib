RCV Recovery Theorem result

DefinitionIn an election with $k$ candidates on a $d$-dimensional preference manifold, plurality keeps ordinal information along one axis and ranked-choice voting keeps it along $\min(d, k-1)$ axes. The recovery ratios are $1/d$ and $\min(d, k-1)/d$.
ExampleWith six dimensions and five candidates, plurality has ratio \(1/6\), about 17 percent, and ranked choice has \(\min(6, 4)/6 = 4/6\), about 67 percent. With three candidates ranked choice has \(2/6\), about 33 percent.
VolumeGeometric Politics, Volume 10 of the Geometric Series; entry id rcv-recovery-theorem, kind result.
StatusA theorem with a proof written in the chapter, which counts independent pairwise comparisons. The information percentages listed elsewhere in the volume are illustrations. Corrections: none recorded.
Defining equationnone
Assumptions and scope
  • The recovery is ordinal and partial. Cardinal intensity is lost, and the Democratic Irrecoverability Theorem still applies.
  • Positions on dimensions that do not affect any candidate ranking stay invisible, as they are under plurality.
  • Chapters 5 and 15 list 45 percent of information preserved for ranked choice in the five-candidate District 7 example. The formula of this theorem gives 4 of 6 for that case, and the volume does not reconcile the two figures.
  • The Alaska 2022, New York City 2021 and Maine 2018 elections are read through the theorem after the fact. They are not tests of it.
Prior artRanked-choice voting is also called instant-runoff voting.
