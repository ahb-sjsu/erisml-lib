Selective Path Dependence (Proposition 13.3) result

DefinitionWith normative relations modelled by a $D_4$ gauge group on the Hohfeldian square, the order in which two considerations are presented changes the judgment only on cross-type paths, where the factors point toward different vertices. On within-type paths the gate elements commute and the Wilson loop is the identity.
ExampleWith the volume’s actions r taking O to C to L to N to O and s exchanging O with C and L with N, applying r then s to O gives s(C) = O, and applying s then r gives r(C) = L. The two orders disagree, and in the volume’s table 2 of 8 scenarios showed an order effect.
VolumeGeometric Communication, Volume 7 of the Geometric Series; entry id selective-path-dependence, kind result.
StatusA proposition whose support is two of eight scenarios in one protocol. The chapter calls it a structural prediction confirmed by that protocol. Corrections: none recorded.
Defining equationnone
Assumptions and scope
  • The evidence cited is SQND Protocol 2 with 640 evaluations over 8 scenarios. Two scenarios, journalist and teacher, showed path dependence (0.659 and 0.862 in the table) and six did not, with combined chi-squared 72.14 on 16 degrees of freedom.
  • The sorting of the eight scenarios into cross-type and within-type is given after the results table, in the same section.
  • The \(D_4\) group itself rests on a quarter-turn r that cycles the four positions. The chapter says r needs a semantic trigger and is probabilistic, while the correlative exchange s held at 100%.
  • For cross-type paths the proposition says the Wilson loop differs from the identity generically, not always.
