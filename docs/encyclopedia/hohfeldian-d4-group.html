Hohfeldian D4 group result

DefinitionWithdrawn pending restatement under the Klein four-group. The statement was that the four first-order Hohfeldian positions, right, duty, liberty and no-right, are permuted by a correlative rotation $r$ of order 4 and an opposite swap $s$ of order 2 that satisfy $srs = r^{-1}$ and generate the dihedral group of order 8. The volume's note of 2026-11-26 says there is no quarter-turn. The correlative and the opposite are commuting involutions and generate the Klein four-group $V_4$, of order 4.
ExampleWrite c for the correlative and s for the opposite. Applying c then s to right gives duty and then liberty. Applying s then c gives no-right and then liberty. The two orders agree, as they must in the Klein four-group, and no product of c and s cycles the four positions.

Assumptions and scope
  • Geometric Ethics, the founding volume, withdrew the dihedral group for the Hohfeldian positions in its version 1.25 of 2026-11-08 and uses the Klein four-group. This volume records the withdrawal in dated notes of 2026-11-26 in Chapters 2 and 5.
  • The withdrawn rotation \(r\) sends right to duty, duty to liberty, liberty to no-right and no-right to right. The note says it applies the correlative at two positions and the opposite at the other two, and is not generated by Hohfeld’s two operations. The swap \(s\), which exchanges right with no-right and duty with liberty, stands.
  • Withdrawn with it are every statement that needs an element of order 4, the dihedral relation, a group of eight elements, or non-commutativity of the two operations. The corrections record says a \(V_4\) form of the eight-element table needs new mathematics and was not attempted.
  • What stands is what uses only the two commuting involutions. The note lists the tables of correlatives and opposites, the bilateral bond, invariance under the correlative swap and under \(V_4\), and the conservation of bond charge.
Prior artHohfeld's jural correlatives and opposites. The withdrawn group is the dihedral group of symmetries of a square, and the group that stands is the Klein four-group.
