recovery ceiling (about 38 percent) result

DefinitionThe observation that an explicit instruction to ignore a perturbation restores about 38 to 39 percent of the verdicts the perturbation displaced, on both the A1 distractor test and the E2 emotional anchoring test. The volume reads it as a limit of prompt-level control.
ExampleThe A1 warning recoveries the volume prints are about 42, 38, 40, 35 and 37 percent, whose mean is 192 / 5 = 38.4 percent. The E2 mean is printed as 38 percent, with single models from 20 to 73 percent.
VolumeGeometric Cognition, Volume 6 of the Geometric Series; entry id metacognitive-recovery-ceiling, kind result.
StatusA measured mean whose run records are not in the repository. The ledger grades its row for a ceiling near 38 percent exploratory (GC-19, worded there as a divided-attention ceiling), and the architectural explanations are the volume's hypotheses. Corrections: none recorded.

Assumptions and scope
  • It is a mean over five models on two subtasks. Gemini 2.0 Flash recovers 73 percent on E2, which the volume says appears to contradict the hypothesis and explains by a hypothesized second mechanism, recomputation from scratch.
  • The volume calls its account of why the figure is 38 percent speculative, and Chapter 17 lists whether the ceiling can be broken as an open question.
  • The override bound of Technical Appendix 12B is given with a proof sketch. It restates the ceiling as about 62 percent of the System 1 error being committed.
