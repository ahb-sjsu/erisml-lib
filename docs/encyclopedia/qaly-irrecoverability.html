QALY Irrecoverability Theorem result

DefinitionAny continuous function that maps the nine-component clinical-moral attribute vector to one scalar value is not injective, has no left inverse, and yields a value-maximizing path that can differ from the clinical geodesic by more than any amount fixed in advance. For the standard QALY, which depends on the outcomes score alone, all variation on the other eight dimensions is lost.
ExampleThe standard QALY reads the outcomes score only. Two patients with outcomes 0.5, one with trust 0.2 and one with trust 0.9, get the same value, and no function of that value can tell them apart. The kernel has dimension 9 - 1 = 8, which is the volume’s count.
VolumeGeometric Medicine, Volume 8 of the Geometric Series; entry id qaly-irrecoverability, kind result.

Assumptions and scope
  • The proof of non-injectivity uses rank and nullity where the function is differentiable and a dimension count where it is continuous.
  • The unbounded divergence is shown by a constructed three-vertex complex in which the path the scalar prefers crosses a boundary whose penalty can be made as large as wished.
  • Proposition 5.1 gives the case where the scalar is adequate. It needs a decision that activates mainly outcomes, a population homogeneous on the other eight dimensions, and no boundary crossed.
  • The result is about loss of information under the map. That the nine dimensions are the right description of a clinical state is the volume’s posited model.
Prior artThe rank-nullity theorem applied to a map from nine dimensions to one. The volume gives it as the clinical form of the Scalar Irrecoverability Theorem of Geometric Reasoning.
