Divergence of utilitarian and manifold-optimal triage result

DefinitionThe allocation that maximizes total outcomes equals the allocation that minimizes total clinical friction when triage activates only the outcomes dimension and crosses no moral boundary. They differ when a boundary is crossed or another dimension is active, as in ventilator withdrawal, age-based deprioritization and the choice of a lottery between similar prognoses.
ExampleOne ventilator, a current patient and a new patient whose outcome gain from reallocation is worth 8. With an abandonment penalty of 15, the low end of the volume’s typical range, and a trust penalty of 5, withdrawal costs 20 against a gain of 8, so the friction-minimizing allocation keeps the ventilator and the outcome-maximizing one moves it. The figures 8 and 5 are arithmetic from the statement.

Assumptions and scope
  • Both allocations obey the same resource bound. The triage problem is a set of patients, one complex and one goal region per patient, and that bound.
  • In the withdrawal case the allocations differ only when the abandonment and trust penalties exceed the outcome differential between the two patients.
  • The proof is an argument from the form of the edge weight. The penalties it uses are not calibrated, and the trust and justice penalties are not among the five boundaries of Definition 4.3.
  • The three predictions about clinician distress in Section 10.5 are described as consistent with the pandemic literature. No test of them is reported.
