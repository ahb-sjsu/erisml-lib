the 3.5 Decomposition concept

DefinitionThe volume's worked illustration of two students, Alex and Ethan, who carry the same 3.5 grade point average and occupy different learner states. Alex is $(0.45, 0.85, 0.70, 0.80, 0.75, 0.65)$ and Ethan is $(0.80, 0.50, 0.40, 0.55, 0.40, 0.35)$, and a weighted sum dominated by domain knowledge stands for the grade.
ExampleWith the volume’s weights, Alex scores 0.270 + 0.2125 + 0.035 + 0.040 + 0.0225 + 0.013 = 0.593 and Ethan 0.6715. The coordinate differences are 0.35, 0.35, 0.30, 0.25, 0.35 and 0.30, whose squares sum to 0.61. The square root of 0.61 is 0.78, the distance the volume prints.
VolumeGeometric Education, Volume 9 of the Geometric Series; entry id three-point-five-decomposition, kind concept.
StatusA posited illustration with composite students. It is a specific instance of Theorem 5.1 only in the sense that two different states can share one scalar, and the printed weights do not themselves give equal scalars. Corrections: none recorded.

Assumptions and scope
  • Both students are composites and their coordinates are estimated from a biographical sketch. Nothing here is measured.
  • With the printed weights 0.60, 0.25, 0.05, 0.05, 0.03 and 0.02 the two projections are 0.593 and 0.6715, which are not equal. The volume says the projections can equalize with different courses and that the structural fact matters, not the specific numbers. Section 1.4.1 states both facts, that both transcripts read 3.5 and that the illustrative weighting separates the two students by 0.0785.
  • The weighted sum is a stand-in. The volume notes that a real grade point average is also filtered through letter grades and particular exams.
