gate C1, the budget reversal contrast correction

DefinitionGate C1 was drafted to test ledger row GC-34, that under a budget manipulation with metric and ideal held fixed, order reversals concentrate on pairs trading a retained direction against an unresolved one. Its own controls showed the registered contrast does not come from the budget, and the registration was ruled not sealable.
ExampleWith agreement chance p = 0.992 the identity gives a trading reversal rate of \(p^2 + (1-p)^2\) = 0.9841, a within-subspace rate of \(2p(1-p)\) = 0.0159 and a contrast of \((2p-1)^2\) = 0.9683, which is the pilot’s value with nothing left for the budget to explain.
VolumeGeometric Cognition, Volume 6 of the Geometric Series; entry id budget-reversal-gate-c1, kind correction.
StatusGC-34 stays predicted and unsealed. The campaign record counts no gate sealed or run and no ledger class raised or lowered. Corrections: none recorded.
Defining equationnone
Assumptions and scope
  • Rebuilding both pair classes against a random plane reproduced the contrast, 0.9531 at rank 1 and 0.9688 at rank 2 against the registered 0.9683. The record says the retained subspace is doing no work.
  • The contrast is an identity in the chance p that a graded verdict agrees with the fitted metric, equal to \((2p - 1)^2\), because a pair’s class label is itself the prediction.
  • Two instruments failed their gates on the first evaluator, reported distances on arithmetic with a calibration \(r^2\) of 0.0946 and pairwise comparison on position bias with a first-position rate of 0.8175. Those runs were voided and not scored against GC-34.
  • Later sections of the draft record a prediction of the cells that failed, a design that never manipulated a budget, and one candidate signal in order-dependence (15 of 192 trading pairs against 4 of 192, Fisher p = 0.0164) that the draft calls a hypothesis and not a result.
