assessment gauge invariance concept

DefinitionAn assessment A, which maps a learner state x and an instrument I to a score, is gauge-invariant when $A(x, I_1)$ and $A(x, I_2)$ differ by no more than measurement noise for every state and every meaning-preserving change of instrument. The volume names five such changes, of examiner, format, language, time and context.
ExampleA student scores 82 on a free-response form and 74 on a multiple-choice form of the same content, and the noise bound is 3 points. The difference of 8 exceeds 3, so for this pair of instruments the assessment is not gauge-invariant.
VolumeGeometric Education, Volume 9 of the Geometric Series; entry id assessment-gauge-invariance, kind concept.
StatusNamed a theorem in the volume. The statement is a criterion that defines a gauge-invariant assessment, and it is the education form of the Bias Invariance Principle of Geometric Reasoning. Corrections: none recorded.
Defining equationnone
Assumptions and scope
  • It does not require identical performance on every instrument. It requires the measured understanding to be the same once instrument-specific variance is removed.
  • The volume calls it a necessary condition for assessment validity. Variance tied to the instrument is treated as systematic bias and not as noise.
  • Which changes of instrument preserve meaning is posited. The volume’s argument that format changes the score is that formats project onto different learner dimensions.
  • The effect sizes quoted for the five changes are cited from the literature in general terms. The volume ran no study, and Prediction 16.3.2 on format has not been tested.
