Taste-heuristic correspondence concept

DefinitionProposition 11.1. The aesthetic tangent vector at a point, the sense of where a program should go next, is the negative metric-raised gradient of the heuristic $h$ of an A* search toward a goal region, $T^\mu = -g^{\mu\nu} \partial_\nu h$. Trained taste is read as a compiled heuristic.
ExampleWith the identity metric and heuristic h = x squared plus y squared, the gradient at the point (1, 2) is (2, 4), so the taste vector is (-2, -4) and points toward the goal at the origin.
VolumeGeometric Aesthetics, Volume 13 of the Geometric Series; entry id taste-heuristic-correspondence, kind concept.

Assumptions and scope
  • It presupposes the aesthetic search problem of Definition 11.1, with a state, a goal region, a task-dependent cost functional and stratum constraints.
  • The volume calls it a consistency condition between the geometric and the computational reading, not a stipulation. Appendix F does not list it among its formal claims.
  • Real taste is taken to be epsilon-admissible and not strictly admissible. Heuristics whose output depends on irrelevant features of the description are classed as gauge-variant, a symmetry failure.
  • Engagement maximization is a greedy local search with no goal region and no accumulated cost. The volume predicts trapping in a local minimum and identifies that with the filter bubble.
Prior artA* search with an admissible heuristic, and its bounded-suboptimal form (Pohl 1970, Pearl 1984, as the volume cites).
